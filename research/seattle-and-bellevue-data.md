## EDUCATIONAL CONTENT ANGLES — SEATTLE AND BELLEVUE

These are parking facts and local context worth publishing — no SpotShare pitch needed. A property manager in Seattle or Bellevue would find this useful on its own.

**Sightline Institute — "Washington State's Parking Reform Is Already Working" (May 7, 2026)**
One year after SB 5184 was signed, Sightline Institute published a confirmation piece: the law is already producing results. Vancouver, WA converted a city-owned parking lot to 95 affordable homes. Bothell accelerated its own parking reform. WA Dept. of Commerce is finalizing implementation guidance by June 2026. Cities 50,000+ have until January 27, 2027 to comply — but some are moving early.
Content angle: "One Year In: What Washington State's Parking Reform Law Means for Bellevue and Seattle Condo HOA Boards" — factual timeline of how SB 5184's rollout affects buildings that were designed before vs. after the July 2025 effective date. Property managers would find this useful to share with their HOA boards.

**Bellevue LUC 20.25A.080 — The Math Behind Bellevue's Visitor Parking Crisis**
Bellevue's Land Use Code requires exactly 1 visitor parking stall per 20 residential units — the minimum. For a 143-unit building like Bosa Park Row, that's 7 required visitor stalls. For a 449-unit development like Main & Bellevue, that's 22. The Urbanist confirmed (January 20, 2026) that Bellevue is actively considering dropping all minimums entirely — which would make future visitor parking supply even more discretionary.
Content angle: A plain-language breakdown for Bellevue condo buyers and HOA boards: "Bellevue's Visitor Parking Math: Why New Luxury Buildings Have Almost No Guest Spots" — using the code numbers, the Bosa projects as examples, and the reform timeline as context.

**Graystone Seattle — $75,000 Per Parking Stall**
The Graystone (271-unit, 30-story, First Hill Seattle, completed 2023) is selling parking stalls for $75,000 each. Only select homes have parking available to purchase. This is not a future scenario — it is the documented current market price for private parking in a Seattle luxury condo building. EV stalls are available but limited. The scarcity is baked into the building's design.
Content angle: "Why Parking in a New Seattle Condo Can Cost More Than a Car" — a factual piece using Graystone's $75,000/stall as the anchor, with SB 5184 as context for why this trend continues for future buildings.
