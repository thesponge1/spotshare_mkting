## EDUCATIONAL CONTENT ANGLES — ST. PETERSBURG, FL

These are parking facts and local context worth publishing — no SpotShare pitch needed. A property manager in St. Pete would find this useful on its own.

**Florida SIRS Reserve Funding Mandate — Deadline Has Passed**
Florida's Structural Integrity Reserve Study (SIRS) mandate now requires full reserve funding as of January 1, 2026. Boards that adopted budgets on or after December 31, 2024 cannot waive reserves. Boards not in compliance are in violation today. Multiple HOA law firms (Falke HOA, ManageCasa, PropFusion, DBPR) have published compliance guides. This is the most financially significant change to Florida condo governance in years — and it puts every Florida HOA board in cost-cutting mode.
Content angle: A plain-language checklist for St. Pete condo HOA boards: "Is Your Building's Reserve Funding SIRS-Compliant? What Florida Condo Boards Need to Know in 2026" — framed as a compliance resource, not a pitch. Property managers across the state are sending this to their clients.

**Florida HB 1203 — Enforcement Precedent Still Developing**
HB 1203 (effective July 1, 2024) caps HOA parking fines at $100/violation with strict pre-notice requirements and prohibits HOAs from restricting residents from parking personal vehicles in lawfully designated spaces. Legal community notes that court precedent is still developing — Varnum LLP warned that "associations with public roads enforcing restrictions more severe than local municipality's should be prepared to see owner challenges and potentially litigation." No definitive court rulings confirmed in 2025–2026 search results.
Content angle: "Florida HOA Parking Enforcement in 2026: What HB 1203 Changed and What's Still Unclear" — factual summary for Florida condo property managers covering what they can and cannot enforce, with a note that precedent is still forming.

**SunRunner TEC-Local Overlay — What Parking-Optional Zoning Means for Existing St. Pete Condo Buildings**
The SunRunner TEC-Local Overlay (first approved September 12, 2024; second reading today) doesn't just affect new buildings — it changes the neighborhood context for existing downtown condos. As new buildings along Central Avenue opt out of parking, street parking pressure increases on blocks where existing buildings have minimal guest spots. Reflection St. Pete already has zero garage guest parking (guests use the street). That street supply is about to get more contested.
Content angle: "What St. Pete's Parking-Optional Zoning Means for Your Existing Condo Building" — a guide for HOA boards at One St. Pete, Reflection, and other established buildings that now face more competition for street guest parking as new neighbors build parking-free.
