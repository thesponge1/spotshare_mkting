**Tampa Bay Times / Mass Transit Mag / City of St. Pete Council (September 12, 2024)**
On September 12, 2024, St. Petersburg City Council voted 6-0 to approve the SunRunner TEC-Local Overlay — a transit-oriented corridor along Central Avenue (and within a quarter-mile of SunRunner BRT stops) where all parking minimums are eliminated for new development. The vote was unanimous. Any developer building along Central Avenue can now opt into a zero-parking design. A final adoption hearing to expand the overlay further is scheduled for May 14, 2026. St. Pete had already eliminated parking minimums for residential units ≤750 sq ft in the downtown core in July 2022 — the SunRunner overlay built on that foundation and removed the floor entirely for new transit-corridor buildings.
Content angle: What St. Pete's parking-optional zoning means for HOA boards in buildings along Central Avenue and why the next vote (May 2026) makes it permanent for the entire corridor.

**Steadily / Ansbacher Law / Bryan Law P.A.**
Florida HOA boards lost their most powerful parking enforcement tool on July 1, 2024. Under HB 1203, HOAs can no longer prohibit residents, tenants, or guests from parking personal vehicles (including pickup trucks) in driveways or any legally designated space. Fines are capped at $100 per violation, $1,000 total for continuing violations. Before any fine can be issued, the board must provide 14 days' written notice, wait for a hearing within 90 days, and follow a committee process. The result: the traditional rule-and-fine approach to HOA parking doesn't work the way it used to, and boards that relied on threats of fines to manage guest parking overflow now need a different tool.
Content angle: Florida HB 1203 changed what HOA boards can do about parking violations — here's what you can still do, and why a reservation system beats an enforcement system in 2026.

**Florida HB 1203 (Effective July 1, 2024) — HOA Enforcement Is Now Harder**
Florida HB 1203, effective July 1, 2024, fundamentally changed what HOA boards can do to restrict parking. Personal vehicles and pickup trucks cannot be prohibited from driveways or legally designated spaces. Fines are capped at $100/violation with strict pre-notice requirements. The rule-and-fine enforcement model that most HOA boards relied on is no longer viable. The boards that navigate this best will be the ones that move from enforcement to management — building a system where parking is allocated by reservation rather than policed by restriction.
