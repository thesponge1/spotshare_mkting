## Tier 0 — Educational Content Angles

### 1. Chicago parking just got very expensive — and congestion pricing may be next

**Source:** costcheckusa.com — Parking Cost in Chicago IL [2026 Guide]; chicagoparkingmap.com — Chicago Parking Tax Rates; Bloomberg (March 2026) — NYC-Style Congestion Pricing Could Pay Off for Chicago

Meter rates in the Loop and River North hit $7.75–$9.75/hour for peak demand in 2026. The Central Business District (outside the Loop) runs $4.75/hour from 8am–midnight. Chicago's monthly parking tax is now 41.25% — the highest of any major U.S. city. Daily garage rates range from $18–$48; monthly parking averages $140–$340. Beyond the current rates, Bloomberg reported in March 2026 that Chicago is actively discussing NYC-style congestion pricing — tolling downtown roads to reduce traffic. If implemented, on-street parking demand in the downtown core would increase further.

**Content angle:** "What does it actually cost your guests to park when they visit your downtown Chicago condo in 2026?" — a practical breakdown with the meter rates, tax reality, and what congestion pricing could mean for residents managing guest parking complaints.

---

### 2. Chicago eliminated parking minimums for 75% of the city in September 2025

**Source:** chicago.gov — Parking Reform; chicagocityscape.com; birchwood.law; Bloomberg (August 2025)

Chicago City Council unanimously voted in July 2025 to eliminate minimum parking requirements for any use within 1/4 mile of a frequent CTA bus route or 1/2 mile of a CTA rail station — covering approximately three-quarters of the city. The ordinance took effect September 25, 2025. Underground parking in Chicago costs approximately $67,500 per space; above-ground runs ~$29,000. Eliminating the requirement means new condo buildings going up in River North, Streeterville, the West Loop, and South Loop can now be built with zero assigned parking if the developer chooses. The city's stated goal is reducing parking supply to encourage transit use.

**Content angle:** "Why newer Chicago condos are being built with less parking — and what HOA boards should do about it" — explains the regulatory shift, the cost math behind why developers will skip parking, and what it means for existing buildings trying to manage guest parking without new construction.
