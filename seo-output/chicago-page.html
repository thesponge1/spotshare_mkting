What property managers and HOA boards need to know in 2026

Chicago has some of the most expensive street parking in the country — and as of September 2025, the city stopped requiring developers to build any of it in new residential buildings near transit. River North, Gold Coast, Streeterville, and the South Loop are all in the affected zone. The buildings going up now are being designed without a parking floor. The HOA boards at those buildings will be the first to feel it.

Why Newer Buildings Have Less Guest Parking

Chicago's approach to parking has changed fundamentally. The question is no longer whether the city might reduce parking requirements — it already has.

On September 25, 2025, the Chicago City Council passed an ordinance eliminating minimum parking requirements for properties within 1/4 mile of a frequent bus route or 1/2 mile of a rail station. That coverage is estimated at approximately three-quarters of the city — encompassing every major luxury condo corridor: River North, Gold Coast, Streeterville, South Loop, Lincoln Park, and Wicker Park.

The math developers are doing: Building one underground parking space in Chicago costs approximately $67,500. Without a legal minimum requiring them to build parking, most developers in transit-adjacent neighborhoods will build fewer spaces, or none at all. That cost savings goes into the unit count, the amenity budget, or the developer's margin — not into guest parking for future HOA boards.

How Buildings Are Solving It Without Adding Spaces

Chicago's downtown condo buildings are built around the same structural reality as every other dense urban market: every unit has an assigned parking spot, and none of those spots are set aside for guests. On paper, that looks like a shortage. In practice, it means most of the garage is empty most of the time.

Residents leave for the office. They travel. They use transit. On any given weekday, a significant portion of resident-assigned spots in a River North or Gold Coast condo sit unused. The shared parking model converts that idle inventory into a managed guest amenity: residents opt in to make their spot available during windows they know they won't need it, and neighbors can book it when a guest is coming.

The HOA doesn't need to buy anything, build anything, or renegotiate the garage structure. The amenity is already in the building. What it needs is a system that makes the available spots bookable, handles access, and gives property management a clear view of what's being used and when.

How it works in practice: A resident in a Streeterville high-rise is flying out Monday morning for a work trip. She won't need her spot until Friday. Another resident's college-age son is visiting Tuesday through Thursday. She makes her spot available through the building app; he books it for two nights. No street parking, no $100 garage bill, no complaint to management.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does my Chicago condo building have to provide guest parking?

No — and as of September 25, 2025, most new Chicago condo buildings don't have to provide any parking at all. The city's new ordinance eliminated minimum parking requirements for properties near transit, covering approximately three-quarters of Chicago. For buildings that already exist, guest parking has always been a building decision — governed by the condo declaration and HOA bylaws, not city code. There is no Illinois law requiring a building to reserve spots for guests.

How much does visitor parking cost near Chicago condos right now?

It depends on the neighborhood, but it's expensive everywhere downtown. Street meters in River North and the Loop peak at $7.75–$9.75/hour in high-demand zones. Daily parking garages in Streeterville, River North, and Gold Coast run $35–$52/day. Monthly parking in River North averages $325–$425/month. And since January 1, 2025, the city charges a 23.25% parking tax on daily lot and garage use — one of the highest in the country.

What did Chicago's September 2025 parking ordinance actually change?

The September 25, 2025 ordinance eliminated minimum parking requirements for new residential and commercial development on properties within 1/4 mile of a frequent bus route or 1/2 mile of a rail station. That covers an estimated three-quarters of Chicago — essentially the entire dense urban core. Developers are no longer required to include any parking in their buildings in those areas. Underground parking costs approximately $67,500 per space to build in Chicago, so without a mandate, most developers will build significantly less. HOA boards at new buildings coming online from 2026 onward may be dealing with a guest parking problem by design from day one.

How do HOA buildings manage guest parking without building new spaces?

The approach gaining the most traction in Chicago's luxury condo buildings is a shared resident parking model: residents who aren't using their assigned spot make it available to neighbors' guests through a building-managed platform. No construction, no capital cost to the HOA — just a board amenity vote and a building rollout. SpotShare is one platform built specifically for this model, designed for HOA communities and luxury condos.

What is the new Illinois accessible parking requirement for condo boards?

A 2025 amendment to Section 18.12 of the Illinois Condominium Property Act required all Illinois condo boards to adopt a formal accessible parking policy by April 2, 2025. The policy must include a written procedure for residents to request accessible parking and a board decision timeline of no more than 45 days. Boards that haven't adopted a policy are now out of compliance. This is separate from how the board allocates guest parking, but it placed parking policy formally on the agenda of every Illinois condo association in early 2025.
