# Guest Parking in Downtown San Diego Condos

**What property managers and HOA boards need to know in 2026**

Most downtown San Diego condo buildings were designed with one parking problem nobody thought to solve: what to do with guests.

Every resident has an assigned spot. Nobody has a guest spot. And the street outside now costs $2.50 an hour — more during a Padres game. It's the complaint HOA boards hear most often, and thanks to the city's own parking reform, it's going to keep getting worse.

This page covers what's actually happening with downtown San Diego parking in 2026, what it's costing your residents' guests, and how buildings in the neighborhood are already handling it.

## Why Newer Buildings Have Less Guest Parking (Not More)

The intuitive assumption is that newer condo buildings have better amenities, including parking. In San Diego, the data says otherwise.

In 2022, the city eliminated minimum parking requirements for commercial properties in Transit Priority Areas — dense, walkable neighborhoods served by transit. A follow-on amendment to the municipal parking code took effect March 11, 2026. The city's stated goal is to reduce parking supply citywide to encourage transit use.

The practical result: developers building new condos in TPAs — which includes most of downtown San Diego — are no longer required to build as many spaces as they used to. Buildings going up in East Village, Little Italy, and the Marina District today may have significantly lower parking ratios than the building next door that was built in 2010.

For HOA boards, this means two things:

1. The guest parking problem isn't being solved by new construction — it's being designed out.
2. Buildings that figure out how to maximize their existing parking have a real competitive differentiator for residents who care about hosting guests.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does my downtown San Diego condo have to provide guest parking?**

No — and there's no law requiring it. Most downtown San Diego condos were built with resident-only parking. Every space is assigned to a unit. San Diego's parking reform has made this more common in newer buildings, not less: developers in Transit Priority Areas can now build with fewer total spaces than they used to be required to provide.

**How much does visitor parking cost in downtown San Diego right now?**

Street meters throughout downtown San Diego cost $2.50/hour as of 2025 — double what they were previously. During Padres games, concerts, and events in the East Village, Gaslamp Quarter, and Marina District, the city activates event pricing across roughly 200 blocks: $10/hour. The city is also rolling out dynamic pricing, with rates projected to reach $3.00–$3.50/hour by late 2026.

**Which neighborhoods have the worst guest parking availability?**

East Village, the Gaslamp Quarter, and the Marina District have the highest concentration of downtown condos with zero dedicated guest parking — combined with the highest meter rates and the most frequent event-pricing activations. Little Italy and Cortez Hill face similar constraints with slightly different parking dynamics.

**How do HOA buildings manage guest parking without adding spaces?**

The most common approach in downtown San Diego is a shared resident parking model: residents who aren't using their assigned spot make it available to other residents' guests through a building app. SpotShare is one platform built specifically for this. It requires no new construction and no capital outlay from the HOA — just an amenity vote and a building rollout.
