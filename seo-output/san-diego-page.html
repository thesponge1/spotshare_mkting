Meter rates doubled on January 31st, Sunday enforcement went citywide, and event zone pricing turned a trip to Petco Park into a $40+ parking bill for anyone without a building spot. Why Newer Buildings Have Less Guest Parking

San Diego has been systematically reducing the regulatory floor on parking supply for years. The June 2025 reform package accelerated that trajectory. The result: every new condo built in a Transit Priority Area is designed under rules that give developers maximum incentive to build fewer spaces.

Here's the timeline of San Diego's parking minimum rollbacks and enforcement changes:

📋

January 2022 — TPA commercial reform

Ordinance O-21041 eliminated minimum parking requirements for businesses in Transit Priority Areas and commercial neighborhoods citywide. The first step in a multi-year deregulation sequence.

📅

August 22, 2025 — O-21989 amendments

Amendments to parking regulations took effect outside the Coastal Zone. Coastal Zone approval anticipated in 2026 as part of the 2026 Land Development Code Update.

🏛️

June 2, 2025 — Comprehensive reform package

City Council approved dynamic pricing, seven-day enforcement, extended meter hours to new neighborhoods (North Park, South Park, Normal Heights, College Area), and updated permit fees — accelerating the overall reduction in public parking supply.

🏢

Spring 2026 — 2026 Land Development Code Update

139 proposed amendments (107 citywide, 32 Downtown-specific) moving toward final adoption. New condo developments will be built under revised TPA parking standards that continue to reduce minimum space requirements.

The pattern is consistent: Each round of San Diego parking reform has reduced the minimum supply requirements for new development and increased the cost and scarcity of public parking. New luxury condos going up in Transit Priority Areas right now are being designed under rules where developers can build far fewer spaces than previous generations. The buildings delivering in 2027 and 2028 will have less built-in guest parking than any prior generation of downtown San Diego condos.

California's vehicle homeowner rights add a layer of complexity. Under California Civil Code Section 4000 and related HOA statutes, residents generally have significant protections for parking in spaces designated for their use — and HOA boards face strict procedural requirements before they can fine, tow, or restrict parking. The traditional enforcement model for HOA parking is constrained, and in buildings where guest spaces are scarce, reactive enforcement is the least effective response.

That scarcity is structural — it does not improve as the regulatory environment continues to reduce minimum requirements for new projects.

How Buildings Are Solving It Without Adding Spaces

The shared parking model starts with a counterintuitive insight: even in buildings where guest parking is scarce, much of the garage sits empty at any given time. Residents travel for work. They spend weekends away. They go days without moving their car. The supply exists in the building — it just isn't being made accessible to guests who need it right now.

A shared parking program converts that idle inventory into a managed amenity: residents opt in to make their assigned spot available during windows they know they won't need it. A neighbor's guest can then book it for the night, the weekend, or a few hours during a Padres game. The HOA doesn't need to purchase additional spaces, negotiate with a garage operator, or convert common areas. The supply is already in the building.

How it works in practice: A resident at a downtown San Diego building is flying to Seattle for four days. She won't need her assigned parking space while she's gone. Her neighbor's parents are driving in from Orange County for the weekend — the building's handful of designated guest spots are already spoken for. She makes her space available through the building's shared parking system; they reserve it for three nights. No $25/day garage bill, no frantic text exchange between neighbors, no complaint to the property manager Monday morning.

San Diego's regulatory trajectory makes the case for this model concrete. As the city's 2026 Land Development Code Update reduces parking minimums further and dynamic meter pricing makes street alternatives more expensive, buildings that have activated their idle resident-assigned inventory become measurably more competitive for prospective buyers and residents. A managed guest parking system is no longer a nice-to-have amenity — it's the gap between a building that works and one that generates chronic complaints.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much did San Diego parking rates change in 2025?

Significantly. Street meter rates doubled from $1.25 to $2.50/hour on January 31, 2025, and enforcement now runs 8am–10pm seven days a week including Sundays. The city also activated $10/hour event zone pricing across 200+ blocks in East Village, Gaslamp, and Marina District for Padres games and large concerts. Balboa Park added paid parking (up to $16/day for non-residents) in early 2026. The City Council approved a comprehensive parking reform package in June 2025 that includes dynamic pricing — rates in high-demand areas will rise further as that rolls out. Current projections put base meter rates at $3.00–$3.50/hour by late 2026 or 2027.

Why are newer downtown San Diego condos being built with less parking?

San Diego's 2022 Transit Priority Area ordinance (O-21041) eliminated minimum parking requirements for commercial properties in TPAs. Subsequent amendments (O-21989, effective August 2025) extended those changes. The 2026 Land Development Code Update — with 139 proposed amendments including 32 Downtown-specific ones — is moving toward final adoption in spring 2026 and will further reduce minimum requirements for residential development in transit-served areas. For developers, each parking space in a downtown high-rise costs significant capital to build and operate. With no regulatory floor requiring them to build guest spaces, the default decision is to build fewer. HOA boards at new buildings inherit whatever parking ratio the developer chose — and there is no guarantee guest spaces were part of that calculation.

How do HOA buildings manage guest parking without adding spaces?

The most effective approach is activating the parking supply that already exists in the building. In any condo building with resident-assigned spaces, a portion of those spaces are unused at any given time — residents are traveling, at work, or simply haven't driven in days. A shared parking program creates a system for residents to make their assigned spaces available to guests during those windows. Guests reserve a space in advance through the building system, and the HOA manages the workflow without adding spaces, hiring staff, or contracting with a garage operator. This model works specifically because every assigned space in the building is a potential guest space during the hours its resident doesn't need it.

Does a downtown San Diego condo building have to provide guest parking?

There is no California state law requiring a condo building to designate dedicated guest parking spaces. Whether guest parking exists, how many spaces are designated, and how they are managed is governed by the condo declaration and HOA bylaws. San Diego's TPA parking reforms mean new developments in transit corridors are not required to build dedicated parking at any minimum ratio — guest spaces are whatever the developer decided to build and whatever the HOA decides to manage. Many older downtown buildings have a small number of designated guest spaces; newer buildings under current rules may have fewer or none as a built-in feature.

Why do buildings with assigned parking still have a guest parking problem?

Because having total parking spaces is different from having accessible guest parking at any given moment. In a building where every space is assigned to a specific resident, those spaces are controlled by individual residents — not by the building. A guest cannot simply park in any open-looking space without risking a tow. Without a system to identify which resident-assigned spaces are temporarily available and make them bookable, the building's total parking capacity exists on paper but is inaccessible to guests in practice. That is the gap a shared parking program fills — not by adding spaces, but by making the existing inventory visible and bookable.
