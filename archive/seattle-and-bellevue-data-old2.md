## EDUCATIONAL CONTENT ANGLES — SEATTLE & BELLEVUE

These are parking facts and local context worth publishing — no SpotShare pitch needed.
A property manager in Seattle or Bellevue would find this useful on its own.

**Governor Ferguson / The Urbanist / Sightline Institute / Parking Reform Network**
On May 7, 2025, Washington Governor Bob Ferguson signed SB 5184 — described by housing policy analysts as "one of the most comprehensive parking reform bills enacted in any US state." Effective July 27, 2025, the law prohibits cities with 30,000+ residents from requiring more than 1 parking space per 2 residential units. It bans parking minimums entirely for affordable housing, senior housing, residential units under 1,200 sq ft, and commercial uses in mixed-use buildings. Cities with 50,000+ residents must comply by January 27, 2027. For Seattle and Bellevue — which together account for most of the luxury condo construction pipeline in the Pacific Northwest — this means every new building in a transit corridor can now legally be designed with no required parking.
Content angle: What Washington's SB 5184 means for HOA boards in new Seattle and Bellevue condos — and why it's not going to be reversed.

**SDOT Blog / SpotAngels / WhereiPark**
Parking near a Seattle condo is not cheap — and the rates are dynamic and rising. Evening meters in Capitol Hill's Pike-Pine corridor peak at $8.00/hour — the highest in the city. Afternoon meters in the Commercial Core and First Hill hit $6.50–$7.50/hour. SDOT adjusts rates quarterly; the November 10, 2025 update held 71% of rates flat but raised 14%. Monthly garage parking in the downtown core runs $300–$400+/month. South Lake Union monthly averages $180–$250/month. In Bellevue, monthly garage rates average $169 with indoor covered spaces reaching $273/month. And unlike Chicago or San Diego, Seattle's residential condo market has seen parking stalls priced as luxury real estate in their own right: The Emerald sold individual stalls for up to $100,000; First Light in Belltown listed spaces at $90,000; Graystone on First Hill at $75,000.
Content angle: The true cost of guest parking near Seattle and Bellevue condos in 2026 — and why building parking now sells for six figures.

**Seattle City Council / The Urbanist**
Seattle adopted its One Seattle Comprehensive Plan on December 16, 2025 — nearly a year past the state deadline. The plan creates 30 Neighborhood Centers with upzoned density across the city. Phase 2 zone-level changes are rolling through 2026. Along Frequent Transit Routes, new zoning likely requires zero off-street parking. Since 2012, Seattle has already applied a no-minimum rule to areas within 1/4 mile of frequent transit in urban villages — resulting in 18,000 fewer parking stalls built over five years, saving $500M+ in building costs. Those savings went into unit count and developer margins, not guest parking for future HOA boards.
Content angle: What Seattle's 2025 growth plan means for the guest parking math in new condo buildings — and what HOA boards need to know before they take their first resident call about visitors.
