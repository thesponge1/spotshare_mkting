What property managers and HOA boards need to know in 2026

Most downtown San Diego condo buildings were designed with one parking problem nobody thought to solve: what to do with guests. Every resident has an assigned spot. Nobody has a guest spot. And the street outside now costs $2.50 an hour — more during a Padres game. It's the complaint HOA boards hear most often, and thanks to the city's own parking reform, it's going to keep getting worse.

Why Newer Buildings Have Less Guest Parking

The intuitive assumption is that newer condo buildings have better amenities — including parking. In San Diego, the data says otherwise.

In 2022, the city eliminated minimum parking requirements for properties in Transit Priority Areas — the dense, walkable neighborhoods served by transit that include most of downtown. A follow-on amendment to the municipal parking code took effect March 11, 2026. The city's stated goal is explicit: reduce parking supply citywide to encourage transit use.

The practical result: Developers building new condos in TPAs are no longer required to build as many spaces as they used to. Buildings going up in East Village, Little Italy, and the Marina District today may have significantly lower parking ratios than the building next door built in 2010.

For HOA boards, this means two things:

📉

The supply problem isn't going away

Guest parking isn't being solved by new construction — it's being designed out of new buildings by city policy.

🏆

It's now a competitive differentiator

Buildings that manage existing parking well have a real advantage for residents who care about hosting guests.

How Buildings Are Solving It Without Adding Spaces

The solution most downtown condo buildings land on isn't construction — it's activation. Downtown San Diego buildings were built with a parking structure that, on its face, looks like a shortage: every space is assigned to a unit, and none are set aside for guests. But that structure contains a latent resource that most buildings aren't using.

At any given time, a significant portion of resident-assigned spots are empty. Owners are at work, out of town, or commuting by other means. The shared parking model turns that idle inventory into a managed guest amenity: residents opt in to make their spot available during hours they know they won't need it, and other residents can reserve it for an incoming guest.

The mechanics are straightforward. The HOA doesn't need to purchase or build anything. There's no new infrastructure. The amenity is already in the garage — it just needs a system to make it bookable.

How it works in practice: A resident knows their parents are visiting on Saturday. They check a building app, find an available spot from a neighbor who'll be out of town, and reserve it in advance. The neighbor's spot gets used. The guest has a place to park. No meters, no circling, no HOA complaint.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does my downtown San Diego condo have to provide guest parking?

No — and there's no law requiring it. Most downtown San Diego condos were built with resident-only assigned parking. Every space belongs to a unit. San Diego's parking reform has made this more common in newer buildings, not less: developers in Transit Priority Areas can now build with fewer total spaces than they used to be required to provide. The March 2026 code amendment reinforced this direction.

How much does visitor parking cost in downtown San Diego right now?

Street meters throughout downtown San Diego cost $2.50/hour as of 2025 — double what they were previously. During Padres games, concerts, and events in the East Village, Gaslamp Quarter, and Marina District, the city activates event pricing across roughly 200 blocks: $10/hour. The city is also rolling out dynamic pricing, with rates projected to reach $3.00–$3.50/hour by late 2026.

Which neighborhoods have the worst guest parking availability?

East Village, the Gaslamp Quarter, and the Marina District have the highest concentration of downtown condos with zero dedicated guest parking — combined with the highest meter rates and the most frequent event-pricing activations. Little Italy and Cortez Hill face similar constraints with slightly different parking dynamics.

How do HOA buildings manage guest parking without adding new spaces?

The most common approach in downtown San Diego is a shared resident parking model: residents who aren't using their assigned spot make it available to other residents' guests through a building app. SpotShare is one platform built specifically for this. It requires no new construction and no capital outlay from the HOA — just an amenity vote and a building rollout.
