Why Newer Buildings Have Less Guest Parking

St. Petersburg has been systematically removing the regulatory floor on parking supply for three years. The question is no longer whether this will affect new buildings — it already has, and the policy is expanding.

Here's the timeline of parking minimum rollbacks in St. Pete:

📋

July 2022 — Downtown core

St. Pete eliminated parking minimums for residential units 750 sq ft or smaller in the downtown core. The first step in a multi-year deregulation sequence.

🚌

September 12, 2024 — SunRunner overlay

City Council voted 6-0 to approve the SunRunner TEC-Local Overlay, eliminating all parking minimums for new development along Central Avenue and within a quarter-mile of SunRunner BRT stops.

📅

May 14, 2026 — Expansion hearing

Final adoption public hearing to extend the parking-optional overlay further along Central Avenue. Any buildings approved after this vote will have zero required parking by design.

What it means for HOA boards

New buildings along Central Avenue and transit corridors can be designed with no dedicated guest spaces — and developers have every financial incentive to do exactly that. The HOA boards at these buildings will inherit the guest parking problem from day one.

The pattern is consistent: Every round of parking minimum reduction in St. Pete has been unanimous or near-unanimous. This is not a contested policy — it is the city's deliberate direction. New luxury condos going up in transit corridors right now are being designed under these rules. The buildings delivering in 2027 and 2028 will have fewer built-in guest spaces than any previous generation of downtown St. Pete condos.

Florida's statewide HOA law adds another layer. HB 1203, effective July 1, 2024, prohibits HOAs from restricting residents, tenants, or guests from parking personal vehicles in driveways or lawfully designated spaces. Fines for violations are capped at $100, and boards must provide 14 days' written notice and complete a committee hearing before any fine can be issued. The traditional rule-and-enforcement model for HOA parking is now significantly constrained under Florida law.

How Buildings Are Solving It Without Adding Spaces

The shared parking model starts with a counterintuitive insight: even in buildings where guest parking is scarce, most of the garage is empty most of the time. Residents travel for work. They spend weekends at the beach. They go days without moving their car. The supply exists — it just isn't being made accessible to guests who need it.

A shared parking program converts that idle inventory into a managed amenity: residents opt in to make their assigned spot available during windows they know they won't need it. Neighbors' guests book it when they're coming in. The HOA doesn't need to purchase additional spaces, negotiate with a garage, or convert common areas. The supply is already in the building.

How it works in practice: A resident at 400 Central is spending the long weekend in Tampa. She won't need her assigned spot Friday through Monday. Her neighbor's parents are flying in from Atlanta on Saturday — and the building's guest spots are full. She makes her spot available through the building app; they reserve it for two nights. No $25/day garage bill, no text chain between neighbors, no complaint to management on Tuesday morning.

Florida's HB 1203 makes the case for this model even stronger. Because HOA boards can no longer easily fine or restrict parking, the only durable approach is one that allocates supply proactively rather than policing violations reactively. A reservation system that lets residents share their spots on their terms is exactly what the law pushes boards toward.

And for boards dealing with the SB 4-D reserve funding shock — special assessments, financial strain, residents pushing back on costs — a guest parking program that generates revenue from idle spaces is one of the few HOA amenity decisions that can pay for itself.

Frequently Asked Questions

What did St. Pete's SunRunner overlay change about parking in new condo buildings?

The SunRunner TEC-Local Overlay, approved by St. Pete City Council on September 12, 2024 by a 6-0 vote, eliminated all parking minimums for new development along Central Avenue and within a quarter-mile of SunRunner BRT stops. Developers building in this corridor can now design buildings with zero required parking spaces. A final adoption hearing to extend this overlay further is scheduled for May 14, 2026. Buildings approved after that vote will have even broader access to zero-parking design options.

What did Florida's HB 1203 change for HOA parking enforcement?

HB 1203, effective July 1, 2024, significantly limited HOA enforcement tools. HOAs can no longer prohibit residents, tenants, or guests from parking personal vehicles (including pickup trucks) in driveways or lawfully designated spaces. Fines for violations are capped at $100/violation (maximum $1,000 for continuing violations), and before any fine can be issued, the board must provide 14 days' written notice and complete a hearing within 90 days. The rule-and-fine approach that many boards relied on to manage parking is now significantly constrained under Florida law.

How much does visitor parking cost near downtown St. Pete condos?

Street meters in the downtown core run $1.00–$1.50/hour. City-owned garages charge $1/hour with a $6/day cap during normal hours. Monthly parking in St. Pete averages $226–$233/month, with downtown core commercial passes running $150–$300/month. During events at Tropicana Field, the Pier District, or the Saturday Morning Market, demand pricing pushes daily garage rates to $15–$30+. The low hourly floor creates a false sense of abundant supply — the scarcity shows up in availability, not price, during peak periods near waterfront buildings.

Does a downtown St. Pete condo have to provide guest parking?

There is no Florida state law requiring a condo building to designate guest parking spaces. Whether guest parking exists, and how it is managed, is governed by the condo declaration and HOA bylaws. St. Pete's SunRunner overlay means new transit-corridor buildings are not required to build dedicated parking at all — guest spaces are whatever the developer and the HOA decide to create, manage, and maintain as a building policy.

Why do buildings with "lots of parking" still have guest parking problems?

Because total spaces and available spaces at any given moment are two different numbers. A 301-unit building with 900+ total spaces has residents assigned to most of those spaces — and no infrastructure to show guests which ones are available right now, tonight, for two hours or two days. Without a managed system, the building's parking capacity exists on paper but isn't accessible to anyone who needs it on a transient basis. That's the gap a shared parking program fills: converting resident-assigned but temporarily unused spaces into a bookable guest amenity.
